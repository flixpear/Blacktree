# THE SOUTHERN BLACK DEATHS FEDERAL REVIEW INITIATIVE 
# Blacktree Campaign: Demanding fair independent federal reviews of suspicious and disputed deaths involving Black Americans

We're not asking ordinary citizens to determine whether something legally qualifies as a lynching or hate crime.

We're asking them to say:

"Something about this case deserves federal review."

# OUR CENTRAL COMMAND 

We demand independent federal review of suspicious and disputed deaths involving Black Americans where credible questions exist concerning the classification of the death, adequacy of the investigation, possible racial motivation, or failure to investigate potential criminal civil-rights violations.
The better demand is:

"Investigate whether these deaths constitute racially motivated crimes, civil-rights violations, or failures to properly investigate potential hate crimes."


- Not claiming guilt.  Not claiming every suicide is a homicide.  Instead:  A citizen-led effort demanding independent federal review of suspicious deaths involving Black Americans where families, witnesses, journalists, or community members have raised credible concerns.
- One important distinction: we should not publicly declare that every death was a lynching unless the evidence establishes that. The demand should be for an independent investigation into whether these deaths were properly classified and whether racial motivation or civil-rights violations were overlooked. That makes the campaign much harder to dismiss.

- if there are multiple deaths that communities believe may have been wrongly classified as suicides—especially deaths involving hanging, racial threats, suspicious circumstances, missing evidence, inadequate autopsies, or questionable local investigations—I would not rely on a petition alone.

The strongest approach is to create a documented federal civil-rights/hate-crime referral campaign around the individual cases.

# Who you should contact

1. FBI — immediately

The FBI specifically investigates hate crimes and civil-rights violations. The FBI says victims or witnesses can report hate crimes through its tip system or by calling 1-800-CALL-FBI (1-800-225-5324), and anonymous reporting is possible.

Submit an FBI Tip

For each death, submit the facts separately. Then identify the cases as part of a larger pattern requiring federal review.

2. DOJ Civil Rights Division

This may actually be even more important for what you're describing.

The DOJ Civil Rights Division accepts reports involving civil-rights violations and can investigate or refer matters to other authorities. Its reporting system allows someone to report a violation involving another person, not just themselves.

DOJ Civil Rights Reporting Portal

The DOJ specifically recognizes racially motivated killings as civil-rights matters. Its historical Cold Case Initiative was created to investigate racially motivated murders, working with the FBI and local authorities.

3. Your U.S. Attorney's Office

For each state where these deaths occurred, contact the appropriate U.S. Attorney's Office and ask for the matter to be referred to the DOJ Civil Rights Division/FBI for review.

The goal isn't simply:

"We think this was a lynching."

The request should be:

"We are requesting federal review of multiple deaths involving Black Americans that were classified as suicides or otherwise closed without an investigation sufficient to exclude homicide, racially motivated violence, or civil-rights violations."

That's a much stronger legal and investigative request.

# PHASE 2 — CREATE AN EVIDENCE RATING

This is extremely important.

We don't want the campaign saying:

"All these people were lynched."

Instead, internally we classify cases.

LEVEL 1 — INFORMATIONAL

There are questions, but insufficient evidence to identify a specific investigative deficiency.

LEVEL 2 — DISPUTED

Family/witnesses dispute the official determination or significant inconsistencies exist.

LEVEL 3 — SUSPICIOUS

There are multiple documented circumstances that reasonably warrant additional investigation.

LEVEL 4 — STRONG FEDERAL REVIEW CANDIDATE

There is substantial documented evidence suggesting possible homicide, racial motivation, investigative misconduct, obstruction, civil-rights violations, or systemic investigative failures.

LEVEL 5 — FEDERAL/CRIMINAL REFERRAL

Evidence appears sufficiently serious that the case should be directly referred to federal investigators and/or appropriate prosecutors.

This prevents us from putting every case into one bucket.

That is how you make this credible to journalists, attorneys and federal investigators.
