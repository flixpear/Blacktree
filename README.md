## 🎯 What We Do

The Blacktree Campaign is a **citizen-led effort** demanding independent federal review of suspicious and disputed deaths involving Black Americans.

**We work with:**
- ✅ Families seeking justice
- ✅ Federal agencies (FBI, DOJ)
- ✅ Civil-rights organizations
- ✅ Congress & lawmakers
- ✅ Community investigators

**Our approach:**
- 📊 Evidence-based documentation
- ⚖️ Legal, defensible language
- 👨‍👩‍👧 Family-first protocols
- 🔄 Self-sustaining systems
- 🤝 Community-moderated

---

## 💡 Our Three Demands

### 1️⃣ Independent Federal Review
Federal review of documented cases where credible questions exist about official determinations.

### 2️⃣ Evidence Preservation
Preservation and appropriate examination of investigative, forensic, and digital evidence.

### 3️⃣ Pattern Investigation
Federal evaluation of whether multiple cases reveal systemic patterns of inadequate investigation, discrimination, or failure to investigate potential hate crimes.

## 🔴 How to Take Action

### Fast Track: "Report It" (10-30 minutes)

**No investigation experience needed. Just report the facts.**

#### Step 1: Report to FBI
- **Phone:** 1-800-CALL-FBI (1-800-225-5324)
- **Website:** https://tips.fbi.gov/home
- **Note:** Can report anonymously

#### Step 2: Report to DOJ Civil Rights Division
- **Website:** https://civilrights.justice.gov/report/
- **Note:** Can report anonymously

#### Step 3: Done
Federal investigators will determine if civil-rights violations or hate crimes occurred.

## 🏗️ The 15-Phase Strategy

### Phase 1: Build the Case File
Create a master database documenting each death with:
- Victim information
- Official investigation details
- Physical circumstances
- Evidence & concerns
- All sources cited

### Phase 2: Create Evidence Rating
Internally classify cases:
- **Level 1:** Informational
- **Level 2:** Disputed
- **Level 3:** Suspicious
- **Level 4:** Strong review candidate
- **Level 5:** Federal referral candidate

### Phase 3: Build Public Database
Create database with:
- Searchable case map
- Public documents
- Timeline of events
- Federal referrals made
- Family statements (approved)

### Phase 4: Federal Reporting
Submit case-specific referrals to:
- ✅ FBI (individual cases)
- ✅ DOJ Civil Rights Division
- ✅ FBI/DOJ Pattern Investigation

### Phase 5: DOJ Civil Rights Division
Umbrella referral requesting:
- Individual criminal investigation
- Hate-crime investigation
- Systemic pattern investigation

### Phase 6: Medical Examiner System
Challenge manner-of-death determinations:
- Who made the determination?
- Was homicide adequately excluded?
- State procedures for challenge

### Phase 7: Public Records / FOIA
Request:
- Incident & supplemental reports
- 911 recordings & dispatch records
- Body camera & surveillance footage
- Autopsy reports & toxicology
- Investigative notes
- Evidence inventories
- Case closure documents

### Phase 8: Civil-Rights Organizations
Approach with evidence packets:
- NAACP Legal Defense Fund
- ACLU Racial Justice Program
- Southern Poverty Law Center
- Local civil-rights attorneys
- Law school clinics

### Phase 9: Congressional Campaign
Request specific actions:
- DOJ/FBI review
- Evidence preservation
- Systemic investigation
- Congressional oversight

### Phase 10: The Petition
Three unified demands:
1. Independent federal review
2. Evidence preservation
3. Pattern investigation

### Phase 11: Media Strategy
Narrative progression:
1. "Families Demand Independent Review"
2. "Records Raise Questions"
3. "Civil Rights Groups Asked to Review"
4. "Federal Authorities Asked to Review"
5. "Evidence Indicates Pattern" (if supported)

### Phase 12: Family Protection
Protocol for all families:
- ✅ No private information without consent
- ✅ No names of alleged perpetrators without approval
- ✅ Families can disapprove any statement
- ✅ Dedicated Family Liaison

### Phase 13: Legal Language
**Use:**
- "Disputed death"
- "Suspicious death"
- "Death requiring independent review"
- "Possible racially motivated violence"
- "Potential hate crime"
- "Investigative deficiencies"

**Avoid unless proven:**
- "They were lynched"
- "The police killed them"
- "Cover-up"
- "Conspiracy"

### Phase 14: Organizational Structure

```
Executive Director
├── Investigations Director (case research)
├── Legal Director (attorneys/referrals)
├── Forensic Advisory Team (experts)
├── Civil Rights Director (DOJ/FBI/Congressional)
├── Public Records Team (FOIA/records)
├── Family Liaison (family support)
├── Communications Director (media/social)
├── Data Director (database/analysis)
└── Volunteer Research Team (community)
```

### Phase 15: The First 30 Days

**Week 1:**
- Establish organization identity
- Create infrastructure (email, storage, database)
- Set up case numbering system (e.g., NBRI-2026-001)

**Week 2:**
- Collect first 10-20 cases
- Verify each case independently
- Assign evidence levels

**Week 3:**
- Prepare individual case packets
- Create National Preliminary Findings Report

**Week 4:**
- Launch FBI referrals
- Launch DOJ referrals
- Send congressional letters
- Contact civil-rights organizations
- Launch petition
- Press release & website
- Social media campaign

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

### Getting Started

**Q: Do I have to prove it was a lynching to report a case?**
A: No. Simply report the facts to the FBI and DOJ. Federal investigators determine if civil-rights violations or hate crimes occurred. You're asking: "Does this deserve federal review?"

**Q: How long does the Fast Track take?**
A: 10-30 minutes. Gather basic facts, report to FBI tip line, report to DOJ. That's it.

**Q: Can I report anonymously?**
A: Yes. Both FBI and DOJ allow anonymous reports.

**Q: Does the FBI actually investigate reports from citizens?**
A: Yes. The FBI says victims or witnesses can report hate crimes directly and anonymously. The DOJ receives thousands of complaints yearly.

---

### Involvement

**Q: What if I want to do more research?**
A: Join the Investigative Track. Help research cases, gather public records via FOIA, conduct family interviews, and build evidence packages. Training provided.

**Q: How do I volunteer?**
A: [Create a GitHub Issue](https://github.com/flixpear/Blacktree/issues/new) expressing interest. Community will connect you with research teams.

**Q: What if my state isn't covered yet?**
A: Report directly to FBI/DOJ without waiting. You can also help build the database for your state.

---

### Petitions

**Q: How do I submit or sign a petition?**
A: Visit the [Petitions Database](PETITIONS_DATABASE.md). Sign existing petitions or submit your own via GitHub Issue.

**Q: Why consolidate petitions?**
A: One petition with 50,000 signatures has more impact than five petitions with 10,000 each. Federal authorities see unified, massive public demand.

**Q: What if my petition doesn't reach top 3?**
A: It remains active in the database. Share widely. Reach 100+ signatures to appear on website.

---

### Privacy & Safety

**Q: How does the campaign protect family privacy?**
A: Family protection is our highest priority. We never publish private information without consent. Families can disapprove any public statements.

**Q: Can families opt out after submitting a case?**
A: Yes. Families always come first. They control their own narrative.
