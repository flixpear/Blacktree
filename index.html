Our Three Demands

1

Independent Federal Review

Federal review of documented cases where credible questions exist regarding official determinations.

2

Evidence Preservation

Preservation and appropriate examination of investigative, forensic, and digital evidence.

3

Pattern Investigation

Federal evaluation of whether multiple cases reveal systemic patterns of inadequate investigation or discrimination.

Frequently Asked Questions

What exactly is the Blacktree Campaign? ▼

The Blacktree Campaign is a citizen-led effort demanding independent federal review of suspicious and disputed deaths involving Black Americans. We work with families, federal agencies, civil-rights organizations, and Congress to ensure thorough investigation of cases where credible questions exist about official determinations.

Do I have to prove it was a lynching to report a case? ▼

No. We're not asking you to determine legal classifications. We're simply asking: "Does this case deserve federal review?" If you see a suspicious death involving a Black American, report the facts to the FBI and DOJ. Federal investigators will determine whether civil-rights violations, hate crimes, or other federal crimes occurred.

How long does the Fast Track take? ▼

10–30 minutes. You gather the basic facts about the case, file a report with the FBI tip line, and file a report with the DOJ Civil Rights Division. That's it. You don't need to do additional research or investigation.

What if I want to do more than report a case? ▼

Join the Investigative Track. You can help research cases, gather public records through FOIA requests, conduct family interviews, and build the evidence base. We provide training and support for volunteers. You can contribute as much or as little time as you have available.

Does the FBI actually investigate reports from citizens? ▼

Yes. The FBI specifically says that victims or witnesses of hate crimes can report them directly to the FBI, and reports can be made anonymously. The FBI reviews each report and investigates when warranted. The DOJ says it receives thousands of complaints from citizens each year.

What happens after I report a case? ▼

The FBI and DOJ review the report to determine whether federal civil-rights violations or hate crimes may have occurred. If so, they can initiate an investigation. We also aggregate reports to show federal authorities that there may be systemic patterns requiring broader investigation.

How does the campaign protect family privacy? ▼

Family protection is our highest priority. We never publish private information without consent, we work exclusively with families through a dedicated Family Liaison, and families can approve or disapprove any public statements about their case. Families always come first.

Can I report anonymously? ▼

Yes. Both the FBI and DOJ allow anonymous reports. You don't need to provide your name or contact information. The focus is on the facts of the case, not on who is reporting it.

What if my state isn't listed yet? ▼

We're building the database nationwide. If you know of a suspicious death in your state, you can report it directly to the FBI and DOJ without waiting for our database to expand. You can also contact us to get involved in building the evidence base for your state.
